Recently, following IndianExpress Report or Expose on ECI, there has been lot of fallout be it political, social or legal or judicial- Wait, judicial? Yes, judiciary has been dragged into this matter by many online commentator, activists and political strongman (even lawyers) related to both government and opposition figures for albeit different reasons. I will primarily be focusing on some remarks by Saurav Das and surprise, Prashant Bhushan (especially targeting Hon’ble Justice SC Sharma).1

Saurav Das Rapid fire of tweets#

First tweet#

So First I would be starting with Saurav Das First tweet,2 he said and I will quote:

“This is how the Supreme Court allowed theft of elections in our country. These judges have much to answer, including the location of their spine. It is useless to have such judges who only delay crucial petitions that challenge the rot in our system. The stealing of the young generation’s right to vote for change has been aided, both intentionally and unintentionally by some Supreme Court judges.” 2

First of all it is a great loaded sentence, This was his reponse to a judgement by a two judge SC bench referring the ECI case to a CJI to form a larger Constitutional Bench because Justice Dipankar Datta and Justice SC Sharma arrived at two different conclusions using legal logic.3 The entire argument between the opinion of judges boils down to two main contention point, Justice Dipankar Datta was arguing that this case doesn’t need to be referred to a larger bench because constitutionality of matter has been sufficiently decided in Anoop Baranwal (supra).3 But Justice SC Sharma contends on this point and says that Anoop Baranwal v UOI only made interim provisions till parliament passed an act in 2023, after that the Act of Parliament that is being challenged needs to be challenged on new constitutional grounds and pursuant to Art 145(3) a Constitutional Bench needs to be convened by CJI.3

Apparently this judgement got on nerves of Saurav Das leading to first aforementioned tweet, for to be clear this is the standard administrative process by which a case whether constitutional or any other cases go through and yes this process is slow and yes the process needs to be reformed, infact it needs to be overhauled completely (will write about that a diff time). Saurav Das could have argued about reforms and can have highlighted it but no he buried a legitimate point under conspiratorial framing using words “intentional”, “aided” and “spine” and he also tried to character assassinate the Justices, where he could have raised a really good and legitimate point.

Second Tweet#

Well we all know what the second tweet is going to be about, he is going to target a judge, isn’t he? Well we all could see from a kilometer away that it would obviously be Justice SC Sharma. But oh boy, get ready for the foul language of second tweet4

“Justice Satish Chandra Sharma must understand that people are not idiots to buy your opinion which has just now led to a delay in deciding the constitutionality of Election Commissioners appointments. Your opinion is invalid.” 4

First of all cool down with the language, Saurav, Honble Justice is holding a constitutional position, I know you could have said the same words in a respectful way but chose to use these words for drama. Second the opinion of Justice SC sharma isn’t egregious at all! Let me get some of his opinion and I am going to quote it word by word:

“In my opinion, the arguments advanced on behalf of the respondents by Mr. Venkataramani, learned Attorney General for India and Mr. Mehta, learned Solicitor General are well-founded. The said Act has been recently enacted pursuant to the decision of the Constitution Bench in Anoop Baranwal v. Union of India here is no authoritative decision of this Court till date pronouncing on the constitutional validity of the said Act. A judicial decision in light of what the Constitution provides has to be given by this Court while it is seized of the issue of vires of Section 7 of the said Act and its other provisions. The decision on these writ petitions would be the first of its kind and it is all the more necessary that a reference is made.” 3

Basically learned Justice is saying that he finds merit in arguments of government counsel and he asserts that court hasn’t pronounced any decision regarding the act itself, Court in Anoop Baranwal (supra) only decided what should be done till parliament has passed an act and parliament did pass an act in 2023, Court did not give any ruling or observations about what the Act should contain or be structured (That would be a violation of Separation of powers if Court passed such orders.) So according to him in order to ascertain whether Section 7 is Ultra Vires or not cannot be decided by this bench because the bench has no legal authority to decide it and a constitutional bench must be made to find if Section 7 is vires or not.

“In this context, the learned Solicitor General relied upon the judgment of a Constitution Bench of this Hon’ble Court in the NJAC case, wherein the Court had held that the presence of even the Law Minister in the Collegium for the selection of Judges of the High Courts and the Supreme Court was violative of the doctrine of separation of powers. Building upon the aforesaid constitutional principle, the submission of the learned Solicitor General was that a corresponding degree of constitutional independence must also be recognized for the Executive as well as the Legislature. He submitted that the Committee constituted under the Rules framed under Article 324 deserves to be declared legal though dominated by the members of the executive. The submission was not as a criticism of the Collegium system or regarding the role of the executive in the appointment of Judges. n my opinion, it was a submission to the effect that if the Constitution Bench has held that the presence of outsiders in the collegium as violative of doctrine of separation of powers, a similar independence should be read in the executive also and the rules framed under Article 324 be not declared void on the ground either that it does not have any outsider in the Committee of selection or there is pre-dominance of only executive in the committee of selection.” 3

Again learned Justice is arguing that just like in NJAC, the Court asserted its authority to be independent from grasp of Executive and Legislature, Similarly Executive and Legislature have right to assert their own independence. Even though I personally find this reasoning for an office like ECI flawed for number of different reasons but constitutionally and legally speaking Justice SC Sharma is right, it is basic separation of powers.

“Having read the decision in Anoop Baranwal (supra), my understanding is that it was dealing with the constitutional vacuum and noticing that no law had been enacted and while leaving it to the Parliament to enact the law, the Constitution Bench proposed an ad hoc arrangement whereby the Hon’ble the Prime Minister, the Hon’ble Chief Justice and the Leader of the Opposition were to form an interim committee to select the Election Commissioners. No doubt, to the extent law has been declared under Article 141 is to be applied but whether or not Parliament was justified in not including a neutral selector in the selection committee is an important question arising for the first time before this Court. Since, it is a substantial question of law as to interpretation of the Constitution, which has not been dealt with in any precedent, the plain language of Article 145(3) read with Order 38 of the Supreme Court Rules does not leave any room for a decision on such question finally by the present combination.” 3

“My view on this contention is this. Old or new, whatever be the character of the statute, if validity of such statute or a part thereof is questioned on valid grounds as failing the constitutional test and if this Court has previously not had the occasion to deal with and pronounce on such grounds, a reference would seem to be in order. This contention of the petitioners militates against the mandatory provisions of Article 145(3) of the Constitution read with Order 38 of the Supreme Court Rules and, hence, has failed to impress me.” 3

“Contention raised that the said Act effectively seeks to legislatively overrule Anoop Baranwal (supra), which is impermissible, is again unmeritorious because of the nature of exercise performed by the Constitution Bench. Had it not been an interim arrangement, the consequence could have been otherwise.” 3

“The submission that reference to a Constitution Bench would permit the respondents to launch a challenge to the decision in Anoop Baranwal (supra) and reopen issues settled by it, short-circuiting the reference procedure and, thus, misusing the provisions of Article 145(3) of the Constitution, proceeds on a misapprehension. Fair and free elections being a part of the basic structure has been acknowledged in the precedents relied on and no Court can or would, possibly, doubt it now. But whether the said Act fails the test of free and fair elections has to be independently examined.” 3

Again nothing novel has been said by the Justice in these points, He is basically arguing Anoop Baranwal only proposed an interim arrangement for choosing ECI until parliament passed an Act. After now the Act has been passed the challenge to validity of the Act must now occurs on the ground of that such said Act violates Basic structure of constitution (Free and Fair elections) and a Constitutional Bench must be established to test the validity. Nothing wrong or legally egregious with the opinion of Justice SC Sharma. But this got Saurav Das to react because apparently the case didn’t went the way he wanted and wanted the Justice to rule on his opinion rather than law and wrote “Your opinion is invalid”,4 Even though Justice SC Sharma reached a opposing view that of Justice Dipankar Datta, his reasoning has followed or is grounded in “procedural propriety”, he followed the mandatory referral logic of Article 145(3), but that doesn’t make his opinion any less legally valid, ultimately it is a legal opinion rested on well founded pillars of Constituion and Statutory law.

History#

Let us take Justice V.R. Krishna Iyer, he before serving as Supreme Court judge was Minister of Home Affairs of Kerala under CPI government in 5 April 1957 – 31 July 1959.11 In Indira Nehru Gandhi v. Raj Narain, 1975, Prime Minister Indira Gandhi sought an urgent, absolute stay from the Supreme Court against the Allahabad High Court judgment that disqualified her, to get full context, the CPI was in coalition with Congress at this period of time and would also go on to support imposition of emergency in 1975 initially.12 And guess who was heading the vacation bench to which Indira Gandhi appealed, Justice V.R. Krishna Iyer, now he could have easily granted the stay and left Indira Govt intact, but did he do it? No, Justice Krishna Iyer (sitting as vacation judge) rejected the government’s demand for an unconditional stay, granting only a conditional stay that barred her from voting in Parliament.

Justice K.S. Hegde in Kesavananda Bharati v UOI voted to create the Basic Structure Doctrine, striking down the government’s attempt to assert unfettered power over constitutional amendments, this would cost him his Chief Justiceship leading to his immediate resignation. He was formerly a member of the ruling Congress party and a Rajya Sabha MP (1952–1957) before being elevated to the judiciary. He after resigning would go on to become 7th speaker of Lok Sabha under Janata Government.13

You see and observe these example and you might ask why we didn’t get biased judgement? Well Justices of Supreme Court being Constitutional Judges are bound by an oath which has words “I will duly and faithfully and to the best of my ability, knowledge and judgment perform the duties of my office without fear or favour, affection or ill-will”.14 Now I know, there have been many corrupt Judges who have no respect for the Oath, but according to all public information available, it has been the exception, rather than trend, Justices usually abide by the oath.

Arguably Krishna Iyer and Hegde had a closer reason to issue favor to government due to their own immediate political past. We are alleging in this specific case that Justice SC sharma colluded because his adult’s son’s client, the gymnastics and circumstantial evidence needed to even scratch this claim speak for themselves.

Now the main point I am bringing all these historical examples and the oath and the trends is to invoke a basic assertion “Extraordinary claims require extraordinary evidence” by Carl Sagan,15 as much applicable it is in astronomy or any other science field, it is as much as applicable in this case. Both Mr Prashant Bhushan and Saurav Das are making an extraordinary claim67 and they must provide us extraordinary evidences, not rumors or circumstantial evidences and screenshots connecting strings with each other and then giving a theory to explain all that. These unfounded snippets about alleged corruptions, do us no help except feeding fuel to a already going fire about credibility of SC and fueling more fire by browbeating judges. So in future they do not give adversarial judgements against public opinion of the time. To the point of Justices doing mental gymnastics and putting their own words in amendments and laws passed by a democratic parliament even if legally there is no trouble or malice or doubt and the law is clear, but only because there exists a public pressure which is supported by a majority. As much as Judiciary needs to be shielded from Executive and Legislature, it needs to be also shielded from exceptional public pressure carried by a majority, whose opinion would result in erosion of constituiton and democracy itself, but there exists no such insulation from public pressure in India, allowing significant public figures to browbeat judges into submission and thereby eroding the constitution and laws that they themselves claim to protect.

Legally#

In India there is no law or binding rule of mandatory or automatic recusal, arguably this is the largest blindspot in Indian Judiciary, so I will concede this point. The only statute applies to lawyers that is BCI rule 6,16 Judges usually follow the recommended but not binding RESTATEMENT OF VALUES OF JUDICIAL LIFE adopted by SC in 7th May, 1997,17 it is basically a recommendation. We will refer to clause 4 and clause 7 specifically

Clause 4:

“A Judge should not permit any member of his immediate family , such as spouse, son, daughter, son- in-law or daughter-in-law or any other close relative, if a member of the Bar, to appear before him or even be associated in any manner with a cause to be dealt with by him.” 17

This literally says that if a Judge has a family member practicing law, then such family member cannot appear before the related Judge in any case, BCI rule 6 also state the same thing for the lawyer side but it has statutory teeth.

Clause 7:

“A Judge shall not hear and decide a matter in which a member of his family, a close relation or a friend is concerned.” 17

Now here things get a little spicy, we can go down two paths from here regarding the interpretation of word “concerned”. But still this is not binding but entirely left to conscience of the sitting judge. Indian law treats recusal as an ethical/discretionary norm for the judge himself rather than a hard legal duty.

I concede this point to attack a more flawed point put up by LiveLaw and Prashant Bhushan.89 First let us take Manak Lal v. Dr. Prem Chand (1957)18 established the “reasonable apprehension of bias” basically meaning “had appeared for a party in it, or had a personal/pecuniary interest in its outcome.” I would argue Here, the son isn’t appearing in either case; he holds a standing government-panel empanelment that’s structurally unrelated to whether the ECI Act 2023 is struck down or upheld. A ruling against the Act doesn’t cost the ECI its standing counsel, doesn’t affect the son’s fees, and doesn’t touch his professional standing — the case is about the composition of the selection committee, not about any ECI litigation his son might be handling.

Second I will argue, Standing counsel positions (his elder son also holds panels for CBDT, Income Tax, and his younger son for MP PSC)10 are fixed-term government appointments, not success-fee or per-case arrangements tied to how any single case comes out. The argument by Prashant Bhushan and quoted LiveLaw article “his son’s growth depends on the ECI winning” 89 doesn’t make a lot of sense, losing a constitutional challenge to an appointment law doesn’t threaten the empanelment itself.

Third, the Jaya Thakur case3 is about the validity of a statute Parliament passed after Anoop Baranwal, ECI itself was more of a bystander of this law than a real party pressing its own institutional interest. This attacks and weakens the “judge is deciding a case that benefits the entity his son serves” 89 claim by Prashant Bhushan and quoted LiveLaw article.

Fourth, There is no legal requirement for Justice SC Sharma to recuse, as pointed above there is no Indian Supreme Court ruling holding that a judge must recuse merely because an adult child, in independent practice, holds a government panel brief for an entity that happens to be a beneficiary (not even a principal party) in a constitutional case before the judge.

But again I will state that there is no mandate for judges to be recused, it all depends on conscience of the justices. This truly needs to be mended, by either a law of parliament or binding regulations by SC. Even though I pointed 4 serious flaws with the arguments presented, I will use the phrase “justice should not only be done, but must also be seen to be done”, justice might have been done (I personally believe the decision to refer the case to constitutional bench is good, because it would prevent future malicious litigation by a hurt executive) and is legally correct, but the “seen” part the most essential part was not fulfilled. I also want to be clear that this is a real structural failure, the absence of a binding recusal rule is a legitimate object of criticism regardless of who raises it or why, and it doesn’t become less true because Bhushan happens to have raised it for all the wrong reasons. Bhushan doesn’t stop at ’the system doesn’t guarantee the appearance of impartiality’ premise. He goes further, using that gap to assert an actual quid pro quo and a promised post-retirement post, without offering any substantial evidence for either and instead relying on rumors and gossip. The structural criticism is fair; the leap to personal corruption is not.

Conclusion#

The main conclusion from this entire saga is people need to stop speculating and relying on rumors to justify their goals because a constitutional case in which they had their own political interests didn’t go their way as they wanted. Instead of challenging it through the proper appellate channel, they immediately started posting unwarranted claims on social media that cannot be publicly verified. Other lesson learned by judiciary and government is to communicate their information to public correctly instead of being silent when others accuse you. Special lesson for judiciary as a whole is to do structural reforms and stop the opacity regarding roster management, compulsory or mandatory or automatic recusal guideline framed by Supreme Court or enshrined in law by Parliament. Most important lesson is stop “BrowBeating” just because case didn’t go the expected way we wanted, doesn’t mean we should browbeat the judge using public majority opinion into submission. A judge must rule on basis of law and constitution rather than majority opinion of public, these incidents make future judges compliant to rule on majority public opinion rather than on basis of law, leading to risks for judicial independence. As I said judiciary may have secured independence from Executive or Legislature but they have not secured independence from public. There must be a separation of Public opinion (most of the time is legally incorrect) and the Judiciary which is a creature of Constitution and law and must rule on those grounds only. If these Political commentators like Saurav Das want to get their public opinion into law then they must approach the correct institution that is parliament to show their public support and get law passed, instead of appearing in front of unelected judges and demanding those judges to decide cases on basis of majority public opinion and insert their words into the law that was passed by a democratic Parliament which itself draws its power from public.
