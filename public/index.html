A Food for Thought

We live in a era of rapid misinformation and post truth content, every lies are spread like wildfires by social media users to every corner of the world. Today I want to focus on a specific type of misinformation that has plagued my field, not since advent of social media, but long long ago, since the advent of media itself, yes! I am talking about law. Multiple times I have seen not only social media but traditional media themselves since long time have potrayed the controversial judgements (actually they are not controversial, I am using that term for the sake of framing only) of court in a twisted way. For example let us take a snippet I recently came across, a news media agency (reputed) had published a headline along the lines “Entering woman’s house, lifting her clothes not rape attempt: Jharkhand HC” now this snippet was shared in a subreddit, I stumbled on, and in comments I saw people who have not read a single line of law or a bare act for that matter commenting ills upon the judge, calling out his caste, and other horrendous comments I cannot describe, but the media agency did not clarify that the judgement was absolutely right! I commented and explained it on that post so I am not going to elaborate but it just boils down to difference between “Preparation” and “Attempt”, Under BNS and other Indian laws, unless explicitly stated Preparation is generally not punishable (there are statutory exception like UAPA), and I read the judgement and facts of this case and the evidences submitted by the Prosecution and all of it pointed that the act this man has committed in this case was not Attempt but rather Preparation which is not punishable by law (BNS criminalises Rape and Attempt to Rape only) then how could the judge under what authority would have punished the person? No one in the comments berating the judge and law had that answer, Should the judge have inserted his own thoughts and words into the law and specifically a penal law passed by a democratically elected parliament to punish this guy? (As a lot of people in comment were saying) NO! Absolutely not! Judge was strictly following law laid down by the act of parliament (This is called Strict Construction, it is a legal doctrine Indian courts use usually in criminal cases). The HC was also following a previous judgement of Supreme Court of India (henceforth referred to as SCI) as laid in Tarkeshwar Sahu vs State of Bihar (Now Jharkhand) on 29 sept, 2006, This is called doctrine of stare decisis which makes judgement pronounced by a Higher court binding on a lower court (Also further adopted explicitly in article 141 of Constitution). The problem is that I had just to do all this research (I knew a lot of doctrine from top of my mind but still had to look up the specific facts of case), that should actually had to be done by the media agency that published the report first! Later in small words towards the end they had included just two sentences, I kid you not but two sentences about “overt proximate act” nothing else! Now you can see the problem, the reporting problem, legacy media and Social media both are the biggest offender of publishing misleading headlines, titles, descriptions, in order to attract TRP and views so that they can gain profit or validation or internet clout. This has effects on judiciary, taking a judge’s judgement or words out of context and then use it to criticise, name call, or harras them or their charecter as a person, has serious effects on their lives, this disempowers them and the entire legal community to give out controversial or challenging judgement that has the capabilty to transform fundamental rights or bring transformative justice, but this type of behaviour from broader community only entices the judge to do deference! Deference towards executive, legislature, society, political strongmans, you name it! This is the reason why I have decided to start this blog, I am not advocating for absolute un criticism towards judiciary, I am advocating for valid on point criticism while calling or pointing out the offenders who are spreading legal mis info and a smear campaign against the institution to lower it’s standing and browbeat the judges, that needs to stop. I will not post much, maybe one article per week, sometimes, Mainly either finding the worst misleading title of week and educating about the truth or Explaining legal doctrine used in Indian Laws so that you can yourself point out the misinfo. Not only Indian laws, I would be discussing, sometimes broadly on topic of Common law and British Legal precedents (mainly historical POV) or US constituional law for a comparative analysis. I would conclude or end this article by saying “What has to be done, needs to be done right now”.
