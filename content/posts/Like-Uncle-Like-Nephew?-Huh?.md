From looking at the title of this article alone, one can come to the conclusion about what I am going to talk today. It is about collegium, a unique system where Judges appoint Judges, and defend this opaque, unfair and possibly corrupt system in name of institutional independence. There are many other ways of appointing Judges while keeping the appointment system impartial and maintaining independence of judiciary and these systems have been put into practice and have proven their grit in many democracies around the world like UK, South Africa, EU, Nambia. NJAC even though with its own concerns and shortcomings and could have potentially lead to slight politicization of appointment process, was a right step in the correct direction. Still Supreme court (SC) struck it down on October 16, 2016 and instead voted to maintain the opaque system of collegium by promising unprecedented transperancy that would never ever come. The defense of collegium was that NJAC would politicize the Court eroding it's impartiality and concentrating the appointment power in the hands of few politicians (PM, LOP and Union law Minister) and cutting significant role of judiciary off from appointment process because judges would form a minority in NJAC panel. This argument was put up by SC while strucking down the NJAC, convineintly forgetting that judges still had the power to Veto any names they did not like. The problem was politicians got the same veto in the panel. But it was good thing, if everyone has a veto, then no one has, Politicians and Judges would have been forced to compromise on appointments leading to neutral appointments that do not please any sides. But no SC wanted to keep that veto for themselves and hence struck down NJAC and said they would provide more transperancy on collegium decisions as a promise, which would be broken. Also, the argument SC put that collegium will ensure true judicial independence that NJAC would erode, has finally fallen off, former Judge K Chandru has recently said Executive has got a lot more power over the appointment process by exploiting loopholes in the same collegium system that was supposed to prevent executive influcence.[^1] This all could have been potentially avoided if NJAC system, even with its own flaws and concerns had replaced the undemocratic and opaque system called collegium that is used only in one country in the entire world which is coincidentally also the world's largest democracy. Truly, a tragedy which has also lead to recent lost of trust crisis the court is battling. In this article I will trace the evolution of collegium and the appointment system that existed before it, why was collegium made? and how it got compromised eventually, the recent loss of trust crisis and how court is losing that battle and in conclusion, offer potential reforms, that I think are would work practically.
